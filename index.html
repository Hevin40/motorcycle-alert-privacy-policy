Motorcycle Alert Privacy Policy

Motorcycle Alert Privacy Policy

Effective date: 3 September 2026 Last updated: 8 September 2026

This policy explains what the Motorcycle Alert app collects, why, who can see it, how long we keep it, and how you can get it deleted. It covers both the iPhone app and the Android app. Both apps use the same account and the same server, so most of this policy applies to both. Where the two are different, we say so, and there are several places where they are genuinely different.

Motorcycle Alert is operated from San Bernardino County, California, United States. You can reach us at support@motorcyclealert.com.

The short version

  • Motorcycle Alert works by putting your live GPS position on a map that other users of the app can see. That is the whole point of the app. It is not something we do quietly in the background.
  • In the app, other users see a coloured marker at your position, labelled with your vehicle type only. Your name, username, email address and vehicle nickname are never displayed anywhere in either app.
  • Your account record does travel to nearby users’ phones so that their app can read your vehicle type, and that record contains your name, username and email address. Nothing displays them, but they arrive on the device. Section 5.3 sets this out in full.
  • Our server keeps only your most recent position. Each new position overwrites the last one, so there is no position history on our server. Hazards you report are a separate, permanent record of a coordinate.
  • Road hazards you report are visible to other users, are kept indefinitely, and carry your account ID.
  • We do not sell your data, we do not share it with data brokers, and there are no adverts in the app.
  • You can delete your account from inside the app at any time. Section 11 says exactly what that removes and what it leaves behind.

2. What the app does

Motorcycle Alert shows car drivers when a motorcycle, an emergency vehicle or a city worker is nearby, and lets users mark road hazards on a shared map. To do that, the app publishes your position while you have it open, and reads the positions other users have published.

You need an account because the account is what carries your vehicle type, and your vehicle type is what decides both what the map shows you and how you appear to other people. Without it the app has nothing to work from.

Motorcycle Alert is not an emergency, dispatch or SOS service. The Emergency Vehicle setting is a vehicle category that changes the icon other drivers see. Nothing in the app contacts the emergency services or summons help, and the app must not be relied on in an emergency.

3.2 Your location

The app collects your precise GPS location. The map and the alerts cannot work without it, and if the app cannot get a position it shows a message asking for location access instead of a map. Your account settings, this policy and account deletion stay reachable without it.

While you are signed in and using the app, your device sends your latest position to our server roughly once a second. We store one position record per account, containing your latitude, your longitude, your account ID, the time of the reading, a flag saying whether you are currently publishing, and, once you sign out, the time you signed out.

Each new position replaces the previous one. Our server holds only the last position your device sent, so there is no position history on our server. Note that hazard reports (section 3.3) are separate permanent records of a place you were, and that on Android other users’ positions are written to the phone of whoever sees them (section 3.7).

Background location. Neither app asks for background or “always” location access. On iPhone the app requests “while using the app” only, and stops sending positions when you leave the app. On Android the position service is started when the app opens and the app never stops it, so it is the operating system, not the app, that stops the position flowing once the app is in the background: without background location permission, which the app does not request, Android stops delivering location to a backgrounded app. We would rather describe it that way than claim a control the Android app does not currently have.

Your position is uploaded even if your vehicle type is Automobile, in which case it is not drawn on anyone else’s map. See section 5.2.

3.3 Road hazards you report

If you tap a hazard button, we store a hazard record containing the hazard type (one of Accident, Road Block, Construction, Debris, Traffic Jam or Other), the exact coordinates where you reported it, a flag saying the hazard is active, the time, and your account ID. The description text is generated automatically from the hazard type. You never type anything into a hazard report, and there is no way to attach a photo, a comment or any other content to one.

Please read section 6 before reporting a hazard from somewhere private, like your home.

3.4 Crash reports

Both apps include Google Firebase Crashlytics. If the app crashes, it sends Google a crash report containing the technical details of the crash, your device model, your operating system version, the app version, and an identifier Crashlytics generates for that installation of the app. We do not attach your name, email, account ID or coordinates to it, and nothing in either app links a crash report to your account. As with any internet request, Google receives the IP address the report came from, and an approximate location can be worked out from an IP address.

3.5 Android only: app usage, performance and device identifiers

None of this section applies to the iPhone app. The iPhone app sends nothing to Google Analytics, generates no usage events, has no access to Apple’s advertising identifier, does not use Apple’s App Tracking Transparency framework, and does not track you across other apps or websites.

The Android app includes Google services that the iPhone app does not.

  • Google Analytics for Firebase. The Android app records simple events about how the app is used. The full list is: sign in attempted, sign in succeeded, sign in failed, sign in rejected for an invalid entry, account creation attempted, succeeded and failed, sign out, moving to the map, account, vehicles or preferences screen, map loaded, zoom in, zoom out, my location tapped, a marker tapped, a hazard marker tapped, a hazard reported, a hazard removed, a proximity warning shown (the event name includes the vehicle type of the vehicle you were warned about), a location update while the map is open, location retrieval failed, and location unavailable. The location update event fires on the map’s own refresh, which is several times a second while the map is open, so it is a continuous stream of events while you use the map, not an occasional one.
  • What those events carry. Our app attaches nothing to them: no name, no email address, no coordinates, no account ID. Google Analytics itself adds its own information to every event, which is normal for the service and outside our control: an identifier for your installation of the app, first open and session start events, screen views, your device model and operating system, and an approximate location derived from your IP address.
  • Firebase Performance Monitoring. Records how long screens and network requests take, along with the device model, operating system and app version.
  • Advertising ID and install referrer. The Google Analytics library adds to the Android app the permission to read your Android advertising identifier, and the ability to read Google Play install referrer information, which describes how you arrived at the app from the Play Store. That comes with the library rather than from anything we wrote. With analytics running, that information is available to Google as part of the analytics data. We do not use it for advertising, we do not show adverts, we run no advertising network in the app, and we do not pass it to anyone for advertising.
  • Two libraries that are included but do nothing. The Android build also includes Firebase App Check and Firebase Remote Config. No code in the app calls either of them, so no device attestation is performed and no remote settings are fetched. They are being removed from the next Android build. We mention them only because the libraries are visible in the app package.

3.6 Maps

On iPhone, the map is Apple Maps, so map requests go to Apple. On Android, the map is Google Maps, so map requests go to Google. Both receive the map area you are looking at in order to send you the map tiles.

3.7 What is stored on your own phone

Sign in details. If you turn on “Remember Me”, your username and password are saved on your own device so you do not have to type them each time. On iPhone this uses the system Keychain, which is the encrypted store the operating system provides. On Android they are saved in the app’s own private storage as ordinary text, which is not encrypted by the app; other apps cannot read it on a normal device, but it is not the same protection as the iPhone Keychain, and we would rather say so than describe the two as equivalent. On Android, changing your password on the account screen also writes the new password into that same storage, whether or not Remember Me is on. Improving this is on our list for the Android app.

Other things kept on the device. On Android the app itself saves your username, email address, name, vehicle type, vehicle nickname, whether Emergency is unlocked, your own latest coordinates, the current list of nearby users (their names, coordinates and vehicle types) and the current list of nearby hazards into its own private storage. On iPhone the app itself saves less: your remember me choice, your two range settings and which vehicle you have selected, with your sign in details in the iPhone Keychain, and it keeps the nearby list only in memory while it is running.

Separately from that, on both iPhone and Android our database keeps its own local copy of whatever the app has read from the server, in the app’s private storage. That copy can include the records described in section 5.3. Other apps on your phone cannot read any of it, and all of it goes when you delete the app.

Your preferences. Your view range and your warning range are stored only on your device and never reach our server, so they do not follow you to another phone. On Android they are cleared when you sign out or delete your account. On iPhone they are not cleared by signing out or by deleting your account; they go when you delete the app.

Notifications. The Android app asks for permission to send notifications but does not currently send any. That request is being removed in the next Android build.

5. Who can see your location: the important section

This is the part of the app most worth understanding, so here it is in full. Everything in this section describes what the apps show and do.

5.1 What other users see in the app

When your position is shown to another user, they see a small coloured circle at your coordinates, with a vehicle icon inside it, and a text label. The label says only your vehicle type: “Motorcycle”, “City Workers”, “Emergency Vehicle” or “Automobile”.

No screen in either app displays your name, your username, your email address, your vehicle nickname or your account ID to another user. On iPhone the marker cannot even be tapped. On Android, tapping the marker shows the vehicle type and nothing else. What arrives on their device behind the screen is a separate question, and section 5.3 answers it.

5.2 Who is shown to whom

The map is deliberately not symmetrical, and this surprises people, so read this carefully.

  • Only users registered as Automobile are shown other users. If your vehicle type is Motorcycle, City Workers or Emergency Vehicle, your own map shows you road hazards and your own position. It does not show you any other user. If your vehicle type is somehow missing or unreadable on your account record, both apps treat you as an Automobile for this purpose, so you would be shown other users.
  • Only users who are not registered as Automobile are shown to anybody. If you are registered as an Automobile, your position is still sent to our server every second, but it is never drawn on anyone else’s map.

So, in practice: if you ride a motorcycle, work on the roads, or drive an emergency vehicle, car drivers near you can see where you are. If you drive a car, no other user’s app shows you.

An Automobile driver’s app draws you only if all of these are also true:

  1. You are signed in and your app is publishing your position.
  2. You are within their chosen view range, measured from where they are now, not from what is on their screen. Each user sets this on their own device, between 2 and 10 miles. The default is 2 miles.
  3. Their app has not filtered you out as stale. This depends on their phone, not yours. An iPhone viewer only draws positions written in the last 5 minutes. An Android viewer applies no such check at all. See section 5.4.
  4. On an Android viewer’s phone only, your account record must carry both a name and a vehicle type. If your record has no name stored, Android users do not see you.

Their map refreshes roughly once a second, so when you move, the marker they see moves with you.

There is no invisible mode and no per trip choice. Being signed in with location access granted is what publishes your position. The ways to stop publishing are: sign out, move the app to the background or close it, turn off location access for Motorcycle Alert in your phone’s settings, or delete your account. We are looking at adding a proper control in the app that stops publishing while leaving the rest of the app usable.

5.3 One thing you should know, stated plainly

When a car driver’s app draws your marker, it downloads your account record from our server in order to read your vehicle type. Databases of this kind return the whole record, not the single field the app asked for, so the record that arrives on their device includes your name, your username and your email address. Their app reads your vehicle type and your name from it and displays only the vehicle type. There is no screen anywhere in either app that shows the rest.

Our database keeps a local copy on each device by default, on both iPhone and Android, so that record is also written into the app’s private storage on that person’s phone, along with your coordinates and your vehicle type. Other apps on their phone cannot read it, and it goes when they delete the app.

So the accurate statement is this: your name, username and email address are not shown to other users, but they are sent to the phones of nearby car drivers as part of the record the app downloads, and they are stored there. We would rather tell you that than write a sentence that sounds better and is not true.

5.4 How long you stay on someone’s map after you stop

This depends on the phone of the person looking, not on yours.

Someone looking on an iPhone stops seeing you 5 minutes after the last position your device sent.

Someone looking on an Android phone has no such cut off. If your app stops publishing without signing out, for example because the app was force closed or the phone lost power, your last known position can stay drawn on Android users’ maps until you sign in again and start publishing, or until your account is deleted. Since most users are on Android, you should assume that is what happens. This is a real limitation of the current Android app, we are fixing it, and you should know about it in the meantime.

5.5 Hazards

A hazard is shown to any signed in user, whatever their vehicle type, whose own current position is within their own view range of the hazard. Panning the map somewhere else does not reveal hazards there: it is distance from the user, not the part of the map on screen, that decides.

Any user can remove any hazard marker, including one you reported. On Android, removal works by matching the coordinates of the marker, so if two hazards were ever reported at exactly the same coordinates, a removal can take the other one. Hazard records are kept indefinitely.

6. Things that are worth thinking about before you use the app

  • Your live position is visible to nearby car drivers whenever you are signed in with the app open, unless your vehicle type is Automobile. Those drivers are strangers. That is the product working as intended, but it is your decision to make.
  • If you drive an emergency vehicle, be aware that your position is drawn on the maps of every nearby car driver using the app.
  • A hazard you report is a permanent record of a coordinate, attached to your account ID. If you report a hazard while standing in your own driveway, that coordinate stays on the map. Report hazards where the hazard is, not where you live.
  • The app is not an emergency service and no part of it summons help.

11. Deleting your account, and exactly what that removes

You can delete your account from inside the app. Open the menu, go to Account Settings, and choose Delete Account. You will be asked to confirm, and you may be asked for your password if you have not signed in recently. You do not need to email us, and you do not need to speak to anyone.

If you delete your account from the iPhone app, we remove:

  • your account record, including your name, email address, username, vehicle type and vehicle name
  • all of your vehicle records
  • your last known position
  • every hazard you have reported
  • your sign in account with Firebase Authentication, including your password
  • your saved username and password on that device

Your view range and warning range stay on that iPhone until you delete the app. They are settings only and are never sent to our server.

If you delete your account from the Android app, we remove:

  • your account record
  • all of your vehicle records
  • your last known position
  • your sign in account with Firebase Authentication, including your password
  • everything the app had stored on that device, including your saved username and password and your range settings

but hazards you reported are not removed by the Android app. They stay on the map with your former account ID attached to them. If you have deleted your account from Android and you want your hazard reports removed as well, email us at support@motorcyclealert.com and we will remove them.

What survives deletion on both platforms:

  • If you were the person who registered the Emergency master unlock code (section 3.1), the record of that code keeps your account ID in a “created by” field. Nothing in either app removes it. Email us and we will.
  • Crash reports already sent to Google Crashlytics stay there for Google’s retention period.
  • Google keeps its own operational records and backups of our database for a limited period as part of running the service. We cannot reach into those, and they expire on Google’s schedule.

If you cannot use the in app route, or you want a copy of your data, want something corrected, or want us to delete something the in app deletion does not reach, email support@motorcyclealert.com. We will deal with deletion requests within 30 days.

Our terms require you to be at least 16 years old to create an account. The app does not ask your age and has no age check, so this is a condition of use rather than something the app enforces. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 16. If we find out that an account belongs to someone under 16, we will delete that account and its data, to the same extent described in section 11, and we will remove by hand anything the in app deletion leaves behind.

If you are a parent or guardian and you believe your child has created an account, email us at support@motorcyclealert.com with the username or email address, and we will delete it.

13. Security

We would rather describe what we actually do than make claims we cannot stand behind.

  • All communication between the apps and our server is encrypted in transit using TLS.
  • Passwords are handled by Google Firebase Authentication. They are stored hashed by Google. We never see them and we cannot recover them.
  • The app and its data live on Google Firebase and rely on Google’s infrastructure security.
  • On iPhone, sign in details saved by “Remember Me” are stored in the device Keychain, which the operating system encrypts. On Android they are stored as ordinary text in the app’s own private storage, which other apps cannot read on a normal device but which the app does not itself encrypt. We are improving that.
  • We keep only your most recent position on our server, so there is no position history on it.

No app, no server and no method of transmission is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. If we become aware of a breach affecting your personal data, we will notify you and the relevant authorities where the law requires it.
